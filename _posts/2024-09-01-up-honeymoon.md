9/1/24

---

Been about a week since we got back home from the Honeymoon. Things are going well at work and we are both getting back into the groove of things. I'm looking back with a week's distance on these Honeymoon days now. 
Day 6 was a "Dog Day", meaning we were focused on staying with the dogs and keeping them entertained. We started the day with coffee and eggs, slowly getting around to planning our activities. I ate a hard tack cinnamon toasty by {% include loc.html id="up-trenary-toast-cafe" %} of Marquette, dipping it in my coffee to resuscitate it back into an edible form. We had actually purchased those on our arrival day in {% include loc.html id="up-covington" %} at the nearby gas station/ grocery (3 mins). Not bad! 

!['Dog Day' has to wait for the rain. "Nonsense!" says Boone.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240823_104925.jpg)

We got the dogs around after some rainfall and drove to a nearby trail called {% include loc.html id="up-canyon-falls" %}. This backwater trail follows a river through rocky terrain that cuts up and around the flowing water, making an interesting scene of exposed jagged bedrock overwhelmed by flowing rushing tea colored water. At the end of our journey down the trail, the river opens up into a pool surrounded by 20 foot walls of layered rock. The boxy lips of rock struck me as very climbable but I resisted the urge since I had no gear and we were with the dogs. I'm certain there were routes present. The difficulty would be bouldering with crashpads due to the waters all around, not deep enough for deepwater solo, but not shallow enough to allow for crashpads to not be soaked (at least with the recent rainfall). 

![Okay, possibly closer to 30 foot walls in certain corners.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240823_122221.jpg)

The jutting chunks of rock would peak up from the water, allowing for little footpaths to sprout out of the middle of the pool; a satisfying perspective for viewing the surroundings. Near this pool was a little waterfall that poured in from the direction we arrived from. While not as large as Tahquamenon Falls, the modest falls were nonetheless well worth the walk and much closer to us so we could enjoy their majesty from up close. 

![The 'Falls' part of 'Canyon Falls' in all their glory.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240823_124217.jpg)

On our walk, Boone got a good climbing session of her own in, hopping up 3 foot boulders and trailing around roots and branches with ease; a natural born hiker. Tilly trailed me closely, minding my steps and choice of footing. The hiking shoes I had purchased in Tahquamenon, Fitkicks, were more sock than shoe, and had gotten soaked on days prior, so I had put on the next closest thing for this hike, watershed I had bought while kayaking at my bachelor party. They may sound uncomfortable but my feet adapted to the high touch sensitivity of both options.

![Boone signs her code word – "" –  signalling potential danger on the trail ahead.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240823_123620.jpg)

![Navigating the path down from the trail into the basin at Canyon Falls, as demonstrated by Aubri and Tilly.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240823_122239.jpg)

Anyways, we eventually started back for the car and got back home to relax. I got to work around 3pm, starting the woodfire in the stove for the hot tub, and maintained the fire while writing for Day 4 of this journal. By 8:30, the hot tub had warmed from what felt like near freezing, to 75°F or so. Not necessarily jacuzzi temperature, but it was getting late so we hopped in for a short dip. The delicate twilight sky gave way to stars and silhouette as the neighbor's bug zapper made efficient pops and cracks to indicate its successful function. The convection of cold water into the stove and hot water out led to a small gurgling belch that could be predicted seconds prior by the keenest of ears. The chirps of frogs soon became drowned out by interloping mosquitos here to terrorize. We quelched the stove fire, drained the tub and dashed back inside trembling cold; Type 2 fun! It was time to read and go to bed. 

![An odd inclusion, but three photos of the same spot, I suspect, indicates a spot well loved. Furthermore, this was the only real photo from the trip that had an indication of the hot tub being prepared for use.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240824_095003.jpg)

For our final full day in the UP, Aubri and I decided to go back to {% include loc.html id="up-marquette" %} to absorb more that the town had to offer. We again started the day off with our coffee, and navigated our way back East. Our first stop after arriving was for souvenirs at the local shops. Aubri picked up a batch of spices from {% include loc.html id="up-spice-merchant" %} while I got my mom a box of chocolates from {% include loc.html id="up-donckers" %} and my dad a coffee thermos with bigfoot on it from {% include loc.html id="up-yooper-casual" %}. Then we stopped for more hot drinks at {% include loc.html id="up-trenary-toast-cafe" %}. I got a chai while Aub got some kind of latte, both very good though dampened by our inability to find Aubri's bank card in her purse. We would search the Nissan, after paying with my Discover but wouldn't find anything. I came to the conclusion it was in the Carhartt I had worn the day prior that was back at the bnb, which would end up being the case but, for now, pretend we don't know that for sure. 

![I still regret not getting this Blackrocks Brewery shirt as a souvenir for myself. If anyone reading this has an extra, email me. I'll pay!]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240824_140521.jpg)

Putting the card out of our minds, we started on our proposed voyage; to experience that which we couldn't 2 days prior, starting with a return trip to {% include loc.html id="up-downwind-marquette" %}. Along our travels up {% include loc.html id="up-3rd-st-marquette" %}, we would stop at {% include loc.html id="up-blossom-bird" %}, a cute little boho cafe serving its name-sake beverages. Inside, a wall of miscellaneous paintings, portraits and odd artwork were scattered on the emerald green backdrop. A line of dark fantasy larpers and neckbeards indicated the direction of service. Plushy devils, moths, and other deconstructionist merch adorn the section in front of the register. Aubri got in line as I fetched our seats near the front window where plants, some real, some fake, sat in the sun. The pastel rococo flavor of the location was right up Aubri's alley, and we sat there sipping bubbles and taking it all in. Then, we got back on path to {% include loc.html id="up-downwind-marquette" %} in the hopes I might uncover some more climbing knowledge while in town.

![Our selected plant-covered nook at Blossom Bird Bubble Tea, with a great view of 3rd Street out the window.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240824_132658.jpg)

When we entered this time, no Bill was present, however we did find a few items of note. Looking around the climbing booth, Aubri spotted a handy little book I had previously only heard about online called "A Rock Climber's Guide to Michigan's Upper Peninsula" written in part by Bill Thompson. The same Bill that provided us direction to The Traverse just 2 days prior. Bill apparently has made a name for himself in the Michigan Climbing scene, organizing the storied Michigan Ice Fest, writing guides, acting as a guide for visiting climbers, and generally helping to establish the UP climbing culture. This would later be confirmed by my local gym owner, Ben Waggoner, once I returned home. 

![Here's the entrance to Downwind Sports, complete with hiking shoe display.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240824_135909.jpg)

So, with a fresh copy of Bill's guide and a Downwind Sports hat, I payed the counter clerk and started back on our walk, happy as ever. We'd walk a few shops on 3rd Street and eventually circled back down, past Downwind's outdoor display of approach shoes, and into a very cool brewery called {% include loc.html id="up-blackrocks-brewery" %}, a converted house, transformed into a multidecker microbrewery with a rooftop hangout, stage, merch shop, and various nooks and crannies to investigate. One of the notable discoveries of this location was a mini gallery in a hall of local artist work hanging up on display and for sale. More remarkable than the art was the method of purchasing the art: a QR code to the artist's Venmo. A crude but charming and industrious means to an end. I liked it. I got a glass of Super Deluxe, a german helles style brew that was quite crisp on the sunny day. I finished it and we made our way further down 3rd Street, eventually deciding to get food at {% include loc.html id="up-lagniappe" %}. 

![Blackrocks Brewery has a striking presence from the street, which prompted my visit.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240824_133503.jpg)

I got the local root beer, and a creole penne, while Aub got shrimp creole. It was pretty spice after eating it for a while and so we eventually packed the leftovers and made our way back to the SUV. We snagged some ice at the local gas station to keep the leftovers cool and went back to {% include loc.html id="up-presque-isle" %}. This time, on a Saturday afternoon, the park was packed making parking difficult. We ended up doing a lap around the park, picking up some ice cream, then doing another lap before finding a spot where we could hike back to {% include loc.html id="up-traverse" %} and I could make another attempt. 

![Aubri looking over the Lagniappe menu, trying to decide which dish to get.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240824_153919.jpg)

This time, however, I was too far into the day and still in recovery from prior attempts to get anywhere so we tried going to {% include loc.html id="up-wetmore-landing" %}, which we had tried to do on the previous day but found signs about parking restrictions that seemed to imply a need for a permit. After a talk with Bill, he said that we could park without too much worry from local law enforcement since he had yet to see that law enforced. So we made the trek and started hiking only to get to a beach front where college locals were taking a cold plunge, but no obvious boulders were in sight, and now with the dwindling sunlight and dogs waiting at home, it was time to call it. We started the voyage back home, got the dogs out one last time, and started packing our things for the return trip the following day. 

![Vibes were high at Wetmore Landing despite loss of daylight and no boulders in my periphery.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240824_191316.jpg)

Well, you know the rest. 9 hours of driving. That morning, Aub made a batch of coffee that we poured into her Stanley and we set off. The dogs sat in the back seat while we listened to many Joe Rogan podcasts about aliens, conspiracies, etc. Earlier on in the voyage, I had on John Denver which was pleasant as we winded through the misty forests full of fog and dew. 

![We said goodbye to the warmth of the cabin, leaving it as we found it.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240825_063507.jpg)

By the final 45 minutes of travel the dogs were sick of it. Boone threw up in protest and they were both done and ready to be home. Upon getting home, we unpacked our luggage. It was now around 5:30pm and we would spend the rest of the day preparing for the week's work. I would write a bit here as well; a satisfying end to a great trip. 

![Looking out from Sugarloaf Mountain to see the wilds of the Upper Peninsula in all their splendor. Practically untouched. Growing, flowing, and rolling back towards civilization.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240822_132609.jpg)

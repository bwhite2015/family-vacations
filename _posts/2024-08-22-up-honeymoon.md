Honeymoon - Day 5 - 8/22/24

---

Waking up on Day 3 (Tues), we decided to make it a Ben and Aubri day at {% include loc.html id="up-mackinac-island" %}. We took the dogs on a walk in the morning and started the trek back south. Along the way, I told Aub to get tickets for the ferry that leaves from {% include loc.html id="up-st-ignace" %}, not Mackinaw City. This was primarily to avoid paying the $4 toll again to come back over the bridge but would also prove beneficial in other ways later. 

![View of The Grand Hotel on our voyage over to Mackinac Island.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240820_104811.jpg)

But for now, we will follow us out onto Lake Huron and over to Mackinac Island where we would step out into a Victorian world where that automobile was never invented (though this too, would need further detailed later). Aubri, who had never been to the island, and in fact, never been to the UP prior to this trip, was amazed by the victorian waterfront homes and horsedrawn carriages. While we would stick to walking the Island over the course of our stay, the immersion provided by the presence of the horses would still be appreciated by the both of us. 

![The bustling Lake Shore Drive on Mackinac Island.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240820_111527.jpg)

At this point, we were getting hungry, so we made reservations at the {% include loc.html id="up-pink-pony" %}. The pastel pinks of the locale were perfectly fine but the food was great! I got the Celebration Burger and Aub got the fish tacos. Moving on, we made our way to {% include loc.html id="up-og-butterfly-house" %} on the island, just East past a mission church and up an alley. Here, we saw a huge variety of Butterfly from all over the world. Every color, size and shape you could think of would land on one of us to rest, then fly off to pollinate some flowers within the greenhouse. It was a pretty cool experience. 

![A butterfly landing on my shoulder in the Butterfly House.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240820_122939.jpg)

We then made our way to the Fort on the hill, {% include loc.html id="up-fort-mackinac" %}, to tour the premises. After walking up the path to the fort, we got the chance to witness a cannon firing. Then, we walked from exhibit to exhibit and absorbed as much as we could. Upon exiting the last of the exhibits, we got to view a shooting drill with some of the 'performers' at the fort. 

![View of Mackinac Island from the top of Fort Mackinac.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240820_125941.jpg)

Once that was done, we walked all the way to the {% include loc.html id="up-grand-hotel" %} to see the massive building in the flesh. The 'Titanic' sized building spanned a near immeasurable length and the general pompousness of the atmosphere made for an easy turn around for us. Getting hungry again, we checked out a pizza shop and found it too pricey for our taste ($35 for a pizza). Instead, we decided to check our {% include loc.html id="up-arch-rock" %}, which involved a trek back up the fort, thru the fort, out the back, east down the road, then taking a bike path the rest of the way; about a 20 minute walk. Here before us, the blue waters of Lake Huron were framed by the magnificent arching rocky structure. A beautiful site and well worth the walk. 

![Arch Rock framing Lake Huron's waters in the distance.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240820_162300.jpg)

Upon returning to the point city, we got a bite at {% include loc.html id="up-patrick-doud-pub" %}. Not bad food at all but not crazy good, we scarfed it down and got to the pier. Oh yeah, and one loose end I forgot to tie up: On our way down from the fort, we did in fact, see a motorized vehicle, the exception to the 'No cars' rule mentioned earlier. There was an obvious reason for this exception; that it was an ambulance. It seems that taking a horsedrawn carriage to the hospital only to die on arrival was an irony that no one wants to fall victim to. So "No motorized vehicles"* with fine print… Also, back to the pier, we found a line stretching out past the entrance and down the street a football field in length. This was not good. As we made our way to the back of the line, Aubri questioned why the line could be so long when there were only limited trips left in the day to get back to shore. Here comes "St. Ignace" again. Someone in the same situation as us asked a person in line what the line was for and they confirmed it was the <u>Mackinaw City</u> line; not the line for St Ignace. Thankfully that line was much shorter. So, we got into the correct line, thankful we were paying attention (Aubri was paying attention) and got our ride back. 

##### *One Note: The ship back to Mackinaw City looked about twice as large as the ship back to St. Ignace, likely to accommodate the larger number of passengers.*

Either way, we were happy to be on our way without unwanted delay. We made it back home after about an hour drive. 

![Aubri and I on our way back to Mackinac Island's port city via the Fort Mackinac steps.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/up-honeymoon-2024/IMG_20240820_165029.jpg)
